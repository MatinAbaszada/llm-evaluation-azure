**RQ1.** How do selected Azure-hosted LLMs differ in output quality, latency, and cost across representative tasks?

**RQ2.** Under which conditions do reasoning-enabled models provide enough benefit to justify their additional cost and latency?

**RQ3.** To what extent can a proxy-based optimization layer improve the cost-efficiency of Azure-hosted LLM applications without unacceptable quality loss?


================================================================================
5. DISCUSSION
================================================================================

This chapter interprets the results of Chapter 4 and answers the three research questions. Numerical values are repeated only where needed to support a claim.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Trade-Offs among Standalone Models
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The findings demonstrate that the quality and cost relationship among the six models is not continuous but instead forms two distinct tiers. Macro accuracy of all reasoning-enabled top-tier models (gpt-5.4-pro 93.2%, gpt-5.4 90.8%, o3-mini 83.3%) is over 80%. In contrast, for gpt-4.1, gpt-5.4-mini, and gpt-4.1-mini, macro accuracy ranges from 57% to 68%. The 15 pp gap between the lowest top-tier model (o3-mini, 83.3%) and the highest bottom-tier model (gpt-4.1, 68.3%) is larger than any within-tier variation. This supports the claim that the difference between these two tiers reflects a fundamental difference in model designs instead of being a gradual improvement. 

The spread along the cost axis is substantially greater than that along the quality axis. While there are only 36 pp differences in macro-accuracy, the cost per request differs by approximately 770 times (gpt-5.4-mini at 0.138 m$ versus gpt-5.4-pro at 106.0 m$). As a result, no model Pareto-dominates. gpt-5.4 has a near-frontier accuracy (90.8%) with a moderate cost (5.37 m$) and the lowest median latency (6.1 s), making it closest to the best global option. All these characteristics make it win the economic-reward ranking at the default weights (-0.444) despite not being the most accurate model. gpt-5.4-pro adds only +2.4 pp accuracy over gpt-5.4 at ~20× the cost and ~1.5× the mean latency, illustrating sharply diminishing returns at the frontier and dropping to fifth place in the reward ordering (-0.705).

Mean latency is conventional, with mini models performing faster than their base counterparts (gpt-4.1-mini at 17.4 s versus gpt-4.1 at 28.1 s). However, the median latency shows the opposite, where gpt-4.1 (6.0 s) and gpt-5.4 (6.1 s) are faster than their respective mini models (9.6 s and 8.2 s). These higher means and lower medians show that most bulk requests finish quickly, while a few long-running calls increase the mean. This finding reflects the real-world operation characteristics more than the model quality. In the Azure environment, quota tier and throttling play a more significant role in the latency compared to model quality. This is especially true for reasoning-capable models, which will inherently have a longer tail due to the deliberation process in the reasoning step. Reward-based decisions in production should therefore rely on percentile latencies rather than means.

Regarding the first research question, the results show that differences among Azure-hosted large language models (LLMs) are pronounced and multidimensional, mainly due to cost variation and accuracy requirements, and do not reflect a consistent quality-latency trade-off. The "best" model is application-dependent and can only be identified based on error, latency, and cost weights.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.2 The Case for Reasoning-Enabled Inference
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Reasoning-enabled inference can assist only a small subset of tasks, and only under very specific cost and speed constraints.

o3-mini beats all 4.1 models on procedural tasks (94.1% HumanEval, 94.0% GSM8K) but is less effective than gpt-5.4 and gpt-5.4-pro on knowledge-heavy GPQA (52.8% vs 82–84%). This means that spending more time deliberating helps only when the limiting factor is the process (chains of arithmetic, programming code) and not stored knowledge (advanced-level science). This is consistent with the recent observation that reasoning under output-length pressure does not monotonically improve quality (Empirical Study of LLM Reasoning under Strict Output-Length Constraint).

Reasoning also imposes the heaviest latency tail in the study. Even though the average latency of o3-mini is 65.7s, it reaches 104.1s on MMLU-Pro, and similarly, the average latency of gpt-5.4-pro is 53.1s, while reaching 104.9s on GPQA. Because of such a small number of slow requests, although ranked in 3rd place in the accuracy results, the economic reward of o3-mini becomes the worst of all six models (-0.787). gpt-5.4-pro, the most accurate model, drops from first in accuracy to fifth in reward (-0.705) for the same reason. Each additional accuracy point at the frontier is disproportionately more expensive and time-consuming, and an approximately 100 m$ and 20 s performance gap relative to gpt-5.4 is not offset by a 2.4 percentage point (pp) accuracy gain achieved under default weights.

Therefore, reasoning models justify their additional cost and latency only when the task benefits from chain-of-thought and error weights far more heavily than latency or cost (corresponding to the high-λ_error region in the heatmaps, where gpt-5.4-pro wins cells). This is why they are not an optimal choice for latency-sensitive Azure workloads. In such cases, lighter models such as gpt-5.4 are top-ranked in the macro reward and the decision map.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.3 Effectiveness of Proxy-Based Optimization
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The proxy-style optimization layer (cascade, router, self-consistency) implemented in this study can improve cost-efficiency, but the three strategies differ sharply, and only one of them defeats every standalone model on a substantial region of the (λ_error, λ_latency) decision map.

Cascade is effectively suppressed by the overconfidence exhibited by LLMs. As modern LLMs tend to return high confidence values, the smaller model escalates no more than 1.1% of queries at T=60/75 and 7.3% at most at T=90 across all twelve configurations. This same calibration issue is also documented in the LLM evaluation literature (Systematic Survey on Evaluating LLMs). As a consequence, every cascade behaves like its small model, and none of them wins any cell of the macro decision map. Cascades only emerge as winners in narrow per-dataset regions on MMLU-Pro and GSM8K (chart C6). The threshold parameter effectively has no effect under the current calibration.

Routing yields the best optimization result. Because decisions are made by an external classifier rather than using the model's confidence, escalation rates increase in the routing strategy. The gpt-5.4-mini router escalates ~25% of requests, with ~42% of escalations occurring in GPQA, ~37% in MMLU-Pro, and approximately 22 to 26% in GSM8K. This leads to an increase in the macro accuracy around +15.5–+16.4 pp relative to a small baseline. Macro-accuracy reaches its maximum at 77.0% with gpt-5.4 as the large worker. Its gpt-4.1-mini / gpt-4.1 configuration sets the study's best reward (-0.498), beating the gpt-4.1-mini (-0.582), gpt-5.4-mini (-0.569), and gpt-4.1 (-0.527) standalones, and is the only configuration that wins a substantial region of the macro decision map (chart R3, the diagonal band between the gpt-4.1-mini and gpt-5.4 areas). Router quality has a greater impact than worker quality, such that, while workers remain unchanged, replacing the gpt-4.1-mini router with the gpt-5.4-mini increases accuracy by approximately 14.9 percentage points.

Self-consistency gives a modest reward gain. Choosing the majority answer across three votes increases gpt-4.1-mini by 6.8 percentage points (from 57.6% to 64.4%) and gpt-5.4-mini by 2.6 points (from 60.6% to 63.2%) while being 3 times more costly and with only about a 1.07× slower latency (17.4 to 18.6 s; 18.4 to 19.7 s) through parallel execution. gpt-4.1-mini N=3 reaches -0.543 and dominates the upper-left of chart SC3 while getting most of the gain on MMLU-Pro and GPQA. However, still the Self-consistency does not close the gap to gpt-4.1 standalone (64.4% vs 68.3%).

With 90.8% accuracy, 5.37 m$ and 6.1 s median latency, gpt-5.4 outperforms all proxy optimization methods wherever accuracy is weighted moderately or higher. Optimization pays off only when deployment is constrained to a cheap worker or a fixed budget (Taming the Titans).

Based on all these, the answer to "To what extent can a proxy layer improve cost-efficiency" is that a proxy layer meaningfully improves cost-efficiency when the workload consists of both easy and hard requests, a well calibrated external router is available, and the budget is too limited to use the strongest standalone. Otherwise, parallel self-consistency offers a cheap rise in accuracy for small models, but a cascade approach isn’t feasible with the currently hosted LLMs on Azure unless explicitly calibrated.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.4 Relation to Prior Literature
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The standalone results align with the price and quality heterogeneity observed in the Economic Evaluation of LLMs and confirm that accuracy alone is a poor basis for choosing a deployment criterion.

Results from the router replicate the main finding of Hybrid LLM and Cross-Attention Routing on Azure OpenAI and 5.4-class models, and augment it by demonstrating router selection is more important than worker selection, and that they are penalised for latency in the same reward.

The cascade results are more negative than those in the early cascade literature. Overconfidence of the models, also flagged in the Systematic Survey on Evaluating LLMs and empirically proven in this study, neutralizes confidence-gated cascading.

The Self-consistency results of the experiments align with the previous observation that the gain in marginal accuracy decays as additional samples are drawn, while the cost increases linearly with N. They also support the observation from Taming the Titans that multi-sample inference acts as a cost amplifier, and it's only worthwhile when implemented alongside parallel dispatch.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.5 Implications for Azure Deployment
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

There are a number of practical implications of these results. First of all, when choosing a deployment model, economic reward should be considered alongside accuracy. gpt-5.4 is the standard recommendation for the general workloads, while gpt-5.4-mini is best for low cost requirements where an accuracy level of around 60 percent is enough. For cases requiring the highest accuracy, gpt-5.4-pro is the most accurate choice if the time and price can be justified.  The second finding concerns reasoning-enabled models, which retain a clear but constrained application domain. They are well-suited for low-throughput tasks that require high accuracy, but underperform when the workload is sensitive to latency, and are surpassed by the gpt-5.4 model in such cases.  The third major point is that the learned router strategy is considered superior to the confidence-based cascade strategy because current Azure LLM models require explicit calibration to make the confidence threshold work. Self-consistency is best reserved for cases in which the N inference calls can be dispatched in parallel, and the worker model is small and cheap enough to withstand 3 times the cost. Additionally, Self-consistency addresses noisy error problems, but if the model makes systematic errors, this approach would not work.  The last point is that deployment specifics, such as quotas or region, have a greater impact on latency than the underlying model. Therefore, rather than relying on published averages, production decisions should be based on measured latencies, such as P50 and P95, for a given quota.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.6 Contributions
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This thesis approaches model selection from a different perspective. To fold accuracy, latency, and cost into a single scalar, a composite, reward-based economic model has been introduced, and the tradeoffs between them are illustrated by computing the reward under different λ_error and λ_latency parameters. Through applying this model, six Azure-hosted models across the 4.1 and 5.4 generations, the mini/full/pro tiers, and the standard and reasoning versions were compared on five benchmarks under identical conditions.

Further, cascade, router, and self-consistency techniques are evaluated within the same framework. Results reveal that only the learned routing method outperforms a standalone model, while LLM overconfidence neutralizes the effectiveness of the confidence-based cascading approach. Finally, the paper addresses several cloud-specific constraints, namely throttling, quota-driven latency outliers, and the divergence between mean and median latency, which are decisive in real deployments but usually absent from academic evaluations.
